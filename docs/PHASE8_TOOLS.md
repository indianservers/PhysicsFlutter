### force-acceleration: Force → Acceleration

Net force changes momentum; constant mass gives acceleration.

Equation: ΣF=ma

Assumptions: Inertial frame, constant mass, valid contact conditions.

Investigation: Double force at fixed mass, then double mass.

Source labs: s02-e00, s04-e01.

### work-energy: Work → Kinetic energy

Net work changes kinetic energy.

Equation: Wnet=ΔK

Assumptions: Classical motion; account for every force doing work.

Investigation: Compare positive and negative work with speed change.

Source labs: s05-e00, s05-e01, s03-e03.

### gradient-field: Potential → Field

A potential gradient sets the corresponding conservative field.

Equation: E=−∇V; g=−∇Φ

Assumptions: Electrostatic / Newtonian gravity, exterior source regions.

Investigation: Compare doubled-distance fields with their potentials.

Source labs: s10-e02, s06-e01, s10-e01.

### oscillation-wave: Oscillation → Waves

Coupled disturbances can propagate; local oscillation and propagation differ.

Equation: v=fλ

Assumptions: Linear medium, a defined phase speed.

Investigation: Compare a local oscillator period with fixed-end standing modes.

Source labs: s07-e00, s08-e03.

### interference: Superposition → Interference

Coherent amplitudes interfere before intensity/probability is calculated.

Equation: I∝|ψ1+ψ2|²

Assumptions: Linear superposition; coherent phase; quantum detections are not classical paths.

Investigation: Compare phase shifts in sound, light and matter waves.

Source labs: s08-e02, s09-e05, s15-e02.

### current-field: Current → Magnetic field

A steady straight current produces an azimuthal magnetic field.

Equation: B=μ0I/(2πr)

Assumptions: Long straight wire, exterior region; finite solenoids use a different model.

Investigation: Reverse current and inspect field direction.

Source labs: s11-e02, s11-e03.

### flux-emf: Magnetic flux → Induced EMF

Time-varying linked flux produces an induced EMF with Lenz sign.

Equation: ε=−dΦB/dt

Assumptions: Defined loop orientation; quasi-static model.

Investigation: Move a dipole through a loop and compare approach/recession.

Source labs: s11-e04, s11-e05.

### micro-thermal: Microscopic models → Thermal response

Macroscopic energy balance links heat, work and internal energy; a microscopic interpretation needs model assumptions.

Equation: ΔU=Q−W

Assumptions: Equilibrium thermodynamics, not a literal molecular movie.

Investigation: Compare first-law energy accounting with lumped cooling.

Source labs: s13-e04, s13-e02, s15-e00.

### gravity-orbits: Gravitation → Orbital motion

An inverse-square central force admits Keplerian bound orbits.

Equation: P²=4π²a³/GM

Assumptions: Isolated two-body / frozen-element teaching models; not full ephemerides.

Investigation: Compare user-defined orbits with sourced planetary ellipses.

Source labs: s06-e03, s16-e00.

### spectra-stars: Spectroscopy → Stellar properties

Identified line positions constrain radial motion; continuum shape and line physics carry different information.

Equation: λo/λr=√((1+β)/(1−β))

Assumptions: Vacuum wavelengths; normalized synthetic template is not an observed atmosphere or abundance fit.

Investigation: Compare hydrogen transitions with shifted synthetic Balmer lines.

Source labs: s15-e03, s16-e02.

### first-order: Capacitor charge → Thermal response

Two distinct first-order models share an exponential response shape.

Equation: y=y∞+(y0−y∞)e^(−t/τ)

Assumptions: Constant RC and a valid lumped single-time-constant thermal response.

Investigation: Plot normalized responses against each model’s t/τ.

Source labs: s10-e03, s13-e00.

### Spring SHM / small-angle pendulum

Sources: s07-e00, s07-e01.

Comparable / synchronization: Common physical time in seconds. Spring displacement m and pendulum angle rad are separate. Pendulum small-angle≤0.1rad; no claim of equal frequencies.

Analogy limits: Linear undamped spring and small-angle pendulum only. Large-angle pendula differ; damping/nonlinear forces are omitted.

### Electric / gravitational inverse-square fields

Sources: s10-e01, s06-e01.

Comparable / synchronization: Common radial coordinate in meters outside source exclusions. Electric field N/C and gravitational acceleration m/s² have separate graphs. Positive charge repels a positive test charge; gravity attracts positive mass.

Analogy limits: Exterior point/spherical sources only. Gravity does not have negative mass or electrostatic cancellation analogues here.

### Wave superposition / optical interference

Sources: s08-e02, s09-e05.

Comparable / synchronization: Common controlled phase difference φ, not one physical clock. Classical amplitude envelope m and coherent optical intensity fraction use separate axes. Optical phase is mapped through exact far-field sinθ geometry.

Analogy limits: Equal coherent classical amplitudes and ideal coherent optical slits; no literal mapping of sound energy to photons or particles.

### Capacitor charging / thermal response

Sources: s10-e03, s13-e00.

Comparable / synchronization: Common normalized response time u=t/τ. Physical RC seconds=uRC; thermal seconds=uτthermal are shown separately. Normalized responses dimensionless; actual V and K remain separate.

Analogy limits: Constant RC and constant-sample first-order thermal probe, not every thermal process or nonlinear diode/capacitance.

### Classical motion / matter-wave detections

Sources: s03-e00, s15-e02.

Comparable / synchronization: Common observation time in seconds. Classical position m is deterministic motion; independent identically prepared matter-wave detector events sample a conditional position probability. No quantum trajectory is drawn.

Analogy limits: One free classical particle versus a statistical ensemble of nonrelativistic electrons. Detection probability is not a classical spatial path or a distribution of classical balls.
