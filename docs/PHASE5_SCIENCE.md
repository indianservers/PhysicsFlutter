# Phase 5 electromagnetic models and references

Pure Dart model **`electromagnetism-5.0`**, in `lib/core/physics/electromagnetism.dart`. Flutter paints and controls are consumers of that state. The [per-lab specifications](PHASE5_LAB_SPECIFICATIONS.md), generated from the shipped curriculum, provide every equation, unit, input range, convention, assumption, method, misconception and worked example for the twelve labs. The reference boards are visual guidance, not scientific evidence.

## Constants and coordinates

Adopted CODATA 2022 central values, rounded as published: ε₀=8.8541878188×10⁻¹² F/m, μ₀=1.25663706127×10⁻⁶ H/m; k=1/(4πε₀). Elementary charge e=1.602176634×10⁻¹⁹ C is exact in SI. ε₀ and μ₀ are measured constants after the 2019 SI revision, not both treated as exact. Rounding at this precision is far below these teaching models’ approximation error. Shared standard gravity remains 9.80665 m/s². No conflicting clock/constants subsystem replaces earlier models.

Coordinates are right-handed: +x right, +y up, +z out of the field board. Vectors support three components, dot/cross product and magnitude. Electrostatic sources/probes are sampled in z=0; source fields remain the 3-D point-charge formula. Lorentz B is uniform and along signed z; solenoid axis is x; induction common coil/source axis and receiver normal are +z. Compass angle is radians from +x, with no fabricated orientation for B=0. Inputs, stored configurations and computed quantities use SI. `emNumber` switches to scientific notation for very small/large values rather than rounding them to zero; it does not rescale the scientific state.

## Electrostatics and field representations

Coulomb force on source 2 is kq₁q₂(r₂−r₁)/|r₂−r₁|³. Force on 1 is its exact negative. Either zero charge gives zero force. The centre separation is constrained to .08… .8 m. Shaded finite sphere drawings are explicitly illustrative; no exact interacting-conductor polarization/contact model is asserted. The camera reduces scale only when required to fit a large separation. Force arrows use a displayed pixels/newton factor; source sizes are illustrative.

Electric Field/Potential permit one to three signed sources with independently editable xy positions. Each point source has a .03 m excluded radius, including an uncharged apparatus object. Source separations must be ≥.06 m. Invalid probes/overlapping sources produce a useful message and no scientific reading/trial. There is no arbitrary field softening. E=ΣkqᵢΔrᵢ/rᵢ³; V=Σkqᵢ/rᵢ with zero at infinity; test-force qₜE and potential energy qₜV are separate. The selected test charge never changes E. Electric-field work from a to b is q(Va−Vb), not ΔU. Dipole midpoint V=0 and nonzero E is deliberately supported; equal like charges cancel E instead.

Electric field glyphs are normalized directions, not an undocumented magnitude scale. Twelve trace seeds per nonzero positive source are used; when there are no positive sources, reversed traces seed negatives. RK4 integrates the normalized field at .004 m steps, bounded by 400 steps, source holes, ±.45 m/±.32 m boundaries, tiny motion and near-zero field. Arrow order follows E (positive to negative). Finite-step traces are numerical representations of noncrossing integral curves; pixel-level crossings near unresolved critical points are not a physical claim. Trace count is qualitative, not proportional to charge. The vector grid is 15×11. Potential uses nine explicit contour levels from −1000 to +1000 V on a 36×24 marching-triangle grid; the 37×25 potential vertices are computed once per source configuration, not once per contour level. Hole cells stay blank. Electric Field does not compute unused potential contours, and Electric Potential does not compute unused line traces.

Probe/test-charge edits reuse static source geometry; horizontal path curves are reevaluated at the selected y. Paths have 161 samples, with gaps for invalid positions. Contours/field-line toggles change representations only. A bounded painter cache retains at most 80 immutable trace paths. Actual readouts are always analytic, not inferred from color, line spacing or normalized arrows. Tests compare E to a central difference of V away from singularities and check superposition, signs, exclusions and cache identity.

Electric Potential also displays independent finite-difference −∂V/∂x and −∂V/∂y with h=1 μm, vector error versus analytic E, and a tolerance of10⁻⁷ times the sum of individual source-field magnitudes. That scale remains meaningful at a field-cancellation point; it is not a physical measurement uncertainty. The comparison is omitted if a finite-difference neighbor would enter an excluded source region. Analytic E remains available at every valid probe. Potential and Ex/Ey path graphs use their own units. The numerical check was added after the recorded GPU profiles; those profiles are observations of the stated rendering workloads, not a benchmark of this additional readout.

## DC current and genuine circuit graphs

Current uses a single ideal ohmic loop: I=V/R if closed, otherwise zero, Q=It and P=VI. Source polarity can reverse I. Electron drift is −I/(neA) using explicitly editable carrier density and conductor area; the default n=8.5×10²⁸ m⁻³ is a copper teaching value, not a measured material characterization. The conductor inset multiplies electron displacement by 10000, with illustrative particle count/size. It is not signal propagation. Input edits restart the time/charge origin, rather than mixing histories from different circuits.

The builder supports 1…12 parts and ≤24 wires. Each component has terminals a,b and a stable ID; wire endpoints refer to those exact terminals. Wire union-find produces electrical nodes independently of component layout. Moving a part updates the drawing/saved layout without solving or changing electrical topology. The source’s a terminal is positive; the first source b node is the zero reference. Passive branch voltage/current signs are a−b/a→b.

Modified nodal analysis stamps each resistor/lamp conductance, and adds branch-current unknowns for ideal voltage sources, closed switches and series ammeters. Open switches/ideal voltmeters have zero conduction. An ideal voltmeter reports the difference between its connected nodes, drawing no current. An ideal ammeter has zero burden voltage and must be in series. Partial-pivot Gaussian elimination uses 10⁻¹² pivot tolerance within the documented bounded R/V range; no pseudoinverse invents a solution to an invalid ideal network. Resistors/ohmic lamps require .1…10000 Ω; sources −24…24 V.

Floating/disconnected nodes, absent sources, invalid components/terminals/values, ideal source shorts and singular/redundant/inconsistent ideal constraints are explanatory states with empty meter readings and disabled Record. An isolated component floats; a resistor connected at only one end can legitimately have zero current and a defined potential through that resistor. These are distinct cases. Direct ideal ammeter/source shorts are rejected. Multiple ideal sources in redundant/conflicting parallel constraints are rejected because branch currents are not uniquely defined, even if voltages agree. A source-free passive circuit is outside this powered-builder scope and is explained accordingly.

Node voltages, every branch current/voltage/absorbed power, KCL residual and total power balance are displayed. Negative source power means delivery. Series, parallel and mixed examples exercise the same solver; no visual-template formula is selected. Sweeps use 26 actual source-voltage graph solves. The lamp is explicitly an ohmic resistor with illustrative brightness P/(P+.5 W), not an incandescent thermal model. No AC/semiconductor behavior is implemented.

## Capacitors and continuous RC switching

Parallel square plates use C=ε₀εrA/d, Q=CV, uniform E=V/d and U=Q²/(2C). Gap≤.05√A restricts the negligible-fringing family. No leakage, breakdown or nonlinear dielectric is modeled. In connected mode V is fixed; isolated mode captures and persists Q when disconnecting, and changes geometry without changing that charge. V and energy can change because mechanical work may occur. The supply voltage control is absent when it would be inert in isolated mode. Plate-gap drawing is explicitly exaggerated.

Separate RC charging/discharging uses chosen R,C, initial V₀, and Vc(t)=Vs+(V₀−Vs)e^(−t/RC), I=(Vs−Vc)/R, Q=CVc, U=CVc²/2. Discharge uses Vs=0. Changing RC charge↔discharge takes the last Vc as the new initial value, resets the local time origin and preserves voltage continuously. Direct initial-condition edits intentionally restart a new run. The geometry and RC modes represent separate supported experiments, not an unmodeled instantaneous rewiring transient. Analytic curves span five τ; one display second represents one τ at 1×. Voltage/current graphs use separate units, not mixed unlabeled axes.

## Magnetism and trajectories

Field Lines uses the 3-D ideal dipole μ₀[3(m·r̂)r̂−m]/(4πr³), moment along signed x, sampled in the xy plane. A conservative r<.08 m exclusion includes the rendered bar and near field. It does not claim a real bar magnet’s interior/near field. External line segments are traced with 14 seeds and at most 600 RK4 steps; magnetic lines close through the excluded source region, not at monopoles. Compass alignment is instantaneous ideal local-B alignment, with no Earth field or inertia.

Lorentz is deliberately B-only: F=q(v×B). No unimplemented electric-field control is exposed. Nonrelativistic total speed must be <3×10⁶ m/s. Uniform signed Bz, editable signed charge/mass/three initial velocity components yield circles, straight parallel paths or helices. Magnetic speed and kinetic energy remain constant to roundoff. Radius mv⊥/(|qB|) and period 2πm/(|qB|) are omitted when undefined at qB=0.

The Boris magnetic rotation uses t=qBh/(2m), s=2t/(1+|t|²), v′=v+v×t, v_new=v+v′×s; position advances with (v_old+v_new)h/2. Internal substeps enforce |qB|h/m≤.03. This is a second-order, speed-preserving magnetic integrator; it is not an easing animation. Tests check opposite charge bending, no magnetic work, exact circle-radius geometry, full-period closure, speed preservation, parallel motion and quadratic time refinement. 3-D camera projection/auto-fit is explicitly view-only; azimuth/elevation controls do not change the model. Particle size is exaggerated; velocity/force glyphs are unit directions, with true values separately shown. Trail≤601 points.

Current-Carrying Wire uses B=μ₀I(−y,x,0)/(2π(x²+y²)) outside r≥.005 m. Positive current is out of the board (dot), with counterclockwise B; negative current is into the board (cross). Compass and arrows reverse consistently. The model is an infinite straight exterior wire, with no unsupported interior/finite-end formula. The 101-point 1/r graph excludes the conductor.

Solenoids uses the finite thin-current-sheet axial expression in the per-lab specification, not a constant field everywhere. μ₀NI/L is shown only for L/R≥10 and |x|≤L/4. Air core only; no unsupported material selector. Length, radius and axial probe share a single displayed px/m mapping; distant probes can be labeled outside the current viewport. The drawing shows at most 32 representative turns with actual N/shown-count metadata, and supports meet the actual drawn coil. Its geometry does not claim to resolve every physical winding.

## Induction through a nonsingular finite magnetic source

The bar-shaped moving source is explicitly represented scientifically by a finite coaxial filament current loop of radius a and magnetic moment m=Isπa². This is not an exact real bar-magnet near-field model. Receiver radius R satisfies R−a≥.01 m, so the source filament never intersects the receiver even as z passes through zero. This avoids the invalid dipole-on-integration-surface singularity.

The Neumann integral after integrating one azimuth is

`M(z) = μ₀ a R / 2 ∫₀²π cosφ / sqrt(a²+R²+z²−2aRcosφ) dφ`.

Flux per receiver turn is Φ=IsM. Its analytic derivative is computed using the same quadrature, replacing the integrand by `−z cosφ/(a²+R²+z²−2aRcosφ)^(3/2)`. Composite midpoint quadrature uses 256 nodes; tests refine 32→64→512, compare the derivative with a geometry difference and verify the far dipole limit. This is actual source/receiver loop geometry, not B at the centre times area. Receiver linkage NΦ and open-circuit EMF −NΦ′v are separate; stationary source gives zero EMF. Signed position/velocity/moment controls and the galvometer/EMF readings share the same state.

The receiver normal is +z and positive current/EMF is counterclockwise viewed from +z. A positive source approaching from z<0 with v>0 increases positive flux and induces negative EMF. After central passage the sign reverses. Reversing velocity or moment reverses polarity. The closed-current option is explicitly the ideal R-only limit I=ε/R with zero self-inductance; it is not I=ε. Real-coil transients require L/R much smaller than the motion time; L is not computed and no physical-coil fidelity is claimed where that condition is unknown. The open option has zero induced current. Meter full scale and clipping are labeled. Source radius, receiver radius and axial position affect their corresponding drawing dimensions/position.

## Electromagnets

One uniform cross section A, mean core path l, one gap g, linear educational μr and ideal closed return path give reluctance `(l/μr+g)/(μ₀A)`, B=μ₀NI/(l/μr+g), flux BA and the ideal single-gap estimate F=B²A/(2μ₀). The apparatus depicts a closed return path and armature held at the chosen exaggerated gap. Load weight is shared standard gravity times mass, and the force/weight ratio is an estimate only. No objects levitate or stick independent of state.

Leakage, fringing, bending/nonuniform flux, hysteresis, heating and saturation are neglected. μr is a user-selected educational constant, not a claimed specification for iron/ferrite/steel. |B|>.3 T is rejected as outside this chosen linear teaching regime; .3 T is a declared educational cutoff, not an invented saturation curve. Air comparison substitutes μr=1 in the same reluctance model; it is a mathematical comparison under the same ideal flux-path assumptions, not an exact finite air-coil lifting calculation or a certified rating. The separate air-core solenoid lab supplies a finite on-axis field expression.

## Time, storage, performance and uncertainty

The existing FixedClock advances at 120 Hz with bounded catch-up and supports display speeds .25…2×. EMRun physical factors: Current/Induction 1 s per display second; Lorentz 10⁻⁶ s; RC RC seconds (one τ) per display second. Static electrostatic/magnetic/circuit/geometry labs recompute from input edits and expose no decorative Play. Dynamic models recompute display state every four ticks, record every eight or on explicit inspection, cap history at 301, and refresh at the exact paused tick. Current stops at 30 s, RC at five τ, Lorentz at20 μs, induction at3 s or |z|>.35 m. Parameter/topology edits restart state; view/layout-only edits preserve physics/solves. Navigation, hidden sections, background and disposal stop tickers; resume never restarts them. No audio/network/plugin addition is needed for this phase.

Sessions persist the full scientific model/kind, SI units/parameters, initial conditions, ticks, mode, held charge, complete circuit topology/component values/layout, probe positions, display preferences, notes, guided work and attempts. Trials add detached current-state/history/profile and actual branch/node data. Reopen reconstructs the original inputs and deterministic run, paused. Schema remains 3; quantity/model validation adds Phase 5 identifiers without rewriting earlier bodies. Native SQLite migration and byte-for-byte preservation are documented separately. Completion requires changed input/topology, guided checks, a correct prediction and a valid recorded trial, not opening a page.

Sources/contours/traces are bounded and cached by configuration. Probe/test-charge changes reuse static geometry, immutable trace paths have a bounded 80-entry cache, and moving circuit parts reuses the solved graph. Circuit solving is not in painter/build paths. Numerical error and model limitations remain separate from display scaling. No uncertainty bars or measured calibration are invented; refer to the existing Measurement uncertainty lab for supported analysis.

The renderer batches each immutable contour set into one path, with at most four cached contour paths. Repeated Canvas labels use a shared bounded 192-paragraph cache with disposal on eviction; current positions/readouts are still painted every frame. These reduce avoidable work without changing any scientific values. Initial and final profile measurements are retained rather than asserting a physical-device frame-rate guarantee.

The shared plot renderer previously treated spans below 10⁻¹² as zero and forced time ranges to at least 10⁻⁶. Unit-independent ranges now preserve femtonewton amplitudes and sub-microsecond spans. This fixes a relevant inherited rendering defect without replacing earlier scientific calculations. The Magnetism studio icon now explicitly labels red N/blue S and directs the gap arrows N→S. Before/after captures and remaining art gaps are in the audit report.
